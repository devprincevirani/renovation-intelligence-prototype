You01

You enter the house once

Address, the facts from the listing, and a photo of each room. It saves after every step, so you can finish on another device. Sizes help, but they are optional.

After you submit, there is nothing more to do.

Photo reading02

We read your photos

Each photo is read for rooms, parts, materials and visible condition. Every finding carries how sure we are. Staged or rendered photos count for layout only.

The photo reading never measures, decides or prices. It only looks.

Rules03

We measure

Your typed sizes come first. Then known patterns in the photos, like a row of tiles, then room templates, then typical sizes. Each size carries its source.

A typical size is never shown as a measurement, and it widens the range.

Rules04

We evaluate what is there

Condition and how dated a part looks are scored apart, against Sam's threshold for each part. A house's age never tells us a part's age.

Below the threshold, a part is marked unverified instead of guessed.

Rules05

We decide the work under each goal

For every part, the least invasive action that meets the goal: keep, clean, repair, refinish, replace, add or inspect. Essential, Standard and Premium are worked out together.

Safety concerns are flagged for a professional first.

Rules06

We count the work

Each action becomes tasks with quantities in square feet, linear feet or each. Every task belongs to exactly one trade.

A door painted by the painter is never billed again by the door installer.

Rules07

We price and check

A Low and a High per line, from local installed rates and real products. Then seven checks run on every line before it may show a price.

What a homeowner pays a professional. Never do-it-yourself, never national averages.

You08

You see the result

The price by package is free. The full presentation shows every room, trade and product, what we could not confirm, and guidance on the house's systems.

    Three goals

    A package is a goal, not a list of work

    Work driven by condition stays in every package. What changes is how far each goal goes.

    Essential

    A functional refresh. Fix what must be fixed so the house works and looks cared for.

    Worn carpet stays if sound. Failed hinges are replaced. Walls get paint where needed.

    Standard Default view

    A coordinated update. Modernize dated parts and keep what is sound.

    Oak cabinets refinished, carpet replaced with LVP, new light fixtures in dated rooms.

    Premium

    A higher finish. Better materials and more function, without replacing parts only because they are old.

    New cabinets and quartz tops. A working furnace is still kept, pending an inspection.

    Where we stop

    What an estimate is not

    We plan the work. A contractor, an inspector and the city still have their part.

    • A contractor's bid or a fixed price
    • A home inspection
    • Permit, building code or safety advice
    • A price for the roof, furnace, water heater or panel
    • A claim about anything hidden in walls or floors
    • A single "expected" number
